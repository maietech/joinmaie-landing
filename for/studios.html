For small studios

Leverage for your team, not just a tool for you.

A small studio grows by taking on more projects without adding the same amount of people. The bottleneck is rarely talent. It's the time everyone spends finding files, asking the producer for context, and redoing work that already exists somewhere.

The picture we're designing for

An owner-producer, two to five editors and contractors, several clients, several projects at once. The owner wants every editor to be able to open a project and understand it, without the owner in the middle of every handoff.

What MAIE offers a studio today

  • VerifiedA consistent structure for every project: the media and the saved references, in one private workspace per project.
  • VerifiedDuplicate flagging for images, so the same still isn't stored and searched for three times under three names.
  • VerifiedAutomatic descriptions and scene detection, so an editor new to a project gets a head start on what's in the footage.
  • LimitedAn agent workspace that drafts plans for routine work, for a person to review.

Where MAIE is today, honestly

ExploringMAIE accounts are currently individual, and there is no team plan. Shared studio workspaces, where an owner gives two editors access to the same project library, are what we're exploring next, and we'd rather design them with working studios than guess.
