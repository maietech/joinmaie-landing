For filmmakers & music-video directors

You know the shot exists.

You just don't want to watch six hours of footage to find it. MAIE is being built for filmmakers and music-video directors who do most of the jobs on their own productions, and who need their footage to remember itself.

The everyday reality

  • Camera cards, phone clips, interviews and B-roll spread across several drives.
  • Filenames that stopped meaning anything three exports ago.
  • For music videos: dozens of near-identical performance takes from a single day, and an edit due by the end of the week.
  • Old footage you'd happily reuse, if you could remember what's in it.

The usual workaround is a spreadsheet nobody keeps up to date, or simply your own memory. Both break the moment the project gets big or a second person joins.

What MAIE does for you today

  • Available nowOne home per project. Media, references and notes stay together in a private workspace, so the project's history doesn't live only in your head.
  • Available nowNo more mystery duplicates. Every file is fingerprinted (SHA-256), so MAIE recognizes the same clip under a new name, and a changed file under an old one.
  • Available nowFootage that describes itself. MAIE writes plain-language descriptions of your stills and finds the scene boundaries in your video. For a music-video shoot, that means a long performance file becomes a set of distinct moments, not one block you have to scrub through.
  • Available nowYour references, searchable. Save the treatments, articles and inspiration you collect into the project, and search them by meaning.

What MAIE doesn't do yet

In developmentTyping "the wide shot at golden hour" and getting the clip back. Searching footage by what's in it is the capability we're building toward, and the descriptions and scene boundaries above are its foundation. It is not available today. When it is, this page will say so.

How it fits a filmmaker's workflow

  1. After the shoot: bring the day's media into the project. Duplicates are recognized, not multiplied.
  2. Before the edit: skim MAIE's descriptions and scene breaks instead of scrubbing every file.
  3. During the edit: keep references, notes and decisions in the same project as the footage.
  4. Next year: the project is still there, with its history, when you want to reuse something.

Help shape what we build next
