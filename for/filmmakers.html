For filmmakers & music-video directors

You know the shot exists.

You just don't want to watch six hours of footage to find it. MAIE is being built for filmmakers and music-video directors who do most of the jobs on their own productions, and who need their footage to remember itself.

The everyday reality

  • Camera cards, phone clips, interviews and B-roll spread across several drives.
  • Filenames that stopped meaning anything three exports ago.
  • For music videos: dozens of near-identical performance takes from a single day, and an edit due by the end of the week.
  • Old footage you'd happily reuse, if you could remember what's in it.

The usual workaround is a spreadsheet nobody keeps up to date, or simply your own memory. Both break the moment the project gets big or a second person joins.

What MAIE does for you today

  • VerifiedOne home per project. Media and saved references stay together in a private workspace, so the project doesn't live only in your head.
  • VerifiedFewer mystery duplicates. MAIE groups likely duplicate images (the same file arriving from different sources, or near-identical frames) so you can keep the one you want. Duplicate detection for video isn't available yet.
  • VerifiedFootage that describes itself. MAIE writes plain-language descriptions of your stills and finds the scene boundaries in your video. For a music-video shoot, that means a long performance file becomes a set of distinct moments, not one block you have to scrub through.
  • VerifiedYour references, searchable. Save the treatments, articles and inspiration you collect into the project, and search them by meaning.

What MAIE doesn't do yet

PlannedTyping "the wide shot at golden hour" and getting the clip back. Searching footage by what's in it is planned, and the descriptions and scene boundaries above are its foundation. It is not available today. When it is, this page will say so.

How it fits a filmmaker's workflow

  1. After the shoot: bring the day's media into the project. Likely duplicate stills are flagged, not multiplied.
  2. Before the edit: skim MAIE's descriptions and scene breaks instead of scrubbing every file.
  3. During the edit: keep references, notes and decisions in the same project as the footage.
  4. Next year: the project is still there, with its media and references, when you want to reuse something.

Help shape what we build next

If you've got a drive full of footage and a specific thing you wish you could find in it, tell us about it. Real libraries and real searches decide what we build next.
