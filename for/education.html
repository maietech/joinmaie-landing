For students, educators & programs

Learn how real productions keep their memory.

Film and media students learn to shoot and cut. Fewer learn what professional teams do with everything around the shot: naming, versions, duplicates, references and handoffs. MAIE makes those habits part of how a project works.

For students

  • Keep every project, from first exercise to thesis film, in its own workspace, with its history intact.
  • Let MAIE fingerprint and describe your media, so a senior project's footage is still understandable years later, when it's portfolio material.
  • Practice the workflow you'll meet on a professional set: organized media, tracked versions, and references kept with the work.

For educators

The question we hear is "Can this help my students learn how real production teams manage media?" Today, an individual student or instructor can use MAIE with a free account. What we don't offer yet is anything class-shaped: shared course spaces, instructor views, or institutional accounts.

For media programs

ExploringWe're exploring what MAIE could look like for film, digital media, journalism and communications programs: student project libraries that outlast a semester, and production-workflow practice built into coursework. We don't have institutional partnerships to announce, and we won't imply any. If your program is interested in shaping this with us, start a conversation.

Why MAIE cares about education

Today's students are tomorrow's editors, producers and studio owners. The habits they learn on their first projects are the ones they'll bring to every production after that, and good production memory is easiest to learn before the archive gets big.
